# Craft: from AI clips to a high-end production

The audience is venue owners and promoters. They have to recognise their own night, believe the product works, and want to work with us. Nothing may feel like a generic AI demo. These rules came out of the director review of the first campaign (Oct 2026) and Max's decisions on it.

## Story

- **A feature trailer is fine**, but it is one night at one door: Lotte is the thread, we open and close on her (she shuts the door on a full room before the logo).
- **A human between two UI shots, always.** A short reaction (Daan grinning when his names are in, Robin nodding at the numbers) keeps it from becoming a screen demo.
- **Promoters are never the villain.** Quota, list lock and the audit log are framed as fair and as credit: "your spots, your name", "your names are in", "credit where it's due". No "who put 15 people on the list?", no promoter being blocked as the punchline.
- **Show the money.** Owners care about revenue: the Paid tier with a door price, the PAID label at the door, free vs paid per promoter in Analytics.
- **Never promise what the app does not do.** Check the real screen before writing an overlay (e.g. the door shows the PAID label but no amount yet; the price lives on the Tiers screen).
- **Logic of the place.** A door host never leaves the door during a rush; put the "no signal" moment at the door itself (bottom of a concrete stairwell), not on a walk to the basement.
- **One CTA per audience.** Owners: "Run your next night on PlusOne." Promoters cannot buy: "Ask your venue for PlusOne." Each reel names its audience.

## UI in the edit (no real filming)

1. Always inside a device: a realistic iPhone or iPad mockup (Rotato, an iPhone 16 Pro mockup), never a flat full-screen image.
2. One focal point per screen: a slow zoom to the one element that matters (quota line, PAID label, the price, one audit row), the rest slightly dimmed.
3. At least 2.5 seconds per screen, one message per screen; the overlay and the UI highlight land together.
4. Screen recordings over stills wherever something happens (typing, tapping, a counter going up).
5. Match the flash look: the same LUT and grain over the UI layer, a hint of glare on the glass, the screen slightly brighter than its surroundings. On green screens: light wrap at the edges and the thumb that touches the glass stays on top of the UI.

## Sound

Silence under picture is the fastest AI tell. Generations stay silent; all sound is built in the edit.

| Layer | What |
|---|---|
| Music | One track, dark minimal or melodic techno, 120–124 BPM, instrumental; cuts on the beat, drop on the door closing and the logo |
| Ambience | Outside: rain on a wet street, muffled bass through the door, queue murmur, distant traffic. Stairwell: echo and bass from below. Inside: full room, glasses. Office: bass through the wall |
| Foley | Thumb tapping glass on every tap, footsteps on concrete, a heavy steel door closing, a walkie-talkie click |
| UI | A short soft tick when a counter rises or something confirms; no cartoon beeps |

Music source, in order of preference: a track from a DJ/producer in the network or a pilot venue with written permission for promo and paid ads; otherwise Artlist or Epidemic Sound with a licence that covers paid ads. Never an unlicensed known track, not even "for the animatic".

## Finishing

- Use at most 1.5–3 seconds of any AI clip and cut on action; the longer a clip runs, the more it drifts.
- One grade over everything (AI clips and UI): the same LUT, grain and a touch of halation, so it feels like one camera.
- Generate finals at 1080p and upscale (Topaz is in Higgsfield); 720p is for tests only.
- Allow imperfection: slight handheld sway, a rack focus that lands a beat late. A perfectly smooth gimbal move reads as AI.
- Type moves on the beat, always in the same place inside the safe zone, with one word in lavender.
- Formats from one edit: 16:9, 9:16 and 4:5. That is why every prompt keeps the action in the centre third.

## Process

Build an animatic before spending credits: lay every reference, startframe and app screen on the chosen music as stills with the right durations and the overlays on top. In half an hour it shows whether pacing and story work and which shots are really needed. Then replace still by still with generated clips, people-in-motion shots first.
